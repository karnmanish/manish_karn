Think for Yourself Yog M Creations
BOOK · BY YOG MANISH

Think for Yourself: How to Stay Sharp, Creative and Independent in the Age of AI

A practical, research-grounded field guide to using AI without quietly losing your ability to think for yourself.

AI can draft your emails, solve your problems, and finish your sentences before you've even formed the thought. But what is that convenience quietly costing you? A fast-growing body of research — from MIT Media Lab's "cognitive debt" studies to the discovery of an "Artificial Hivemind" effect narrowing human creativity — suggests the same tools making life easier may be eroding our ability to think critically, create original ideas, and make decisions that are genuinely our own.

Inside, you'll discover:

  • Why "cognitive debt" builds silently — and the MIT brain-scan study that revealed it
  • The Artificial Hivemind effect — why AI-assisted ideas are converging, and how to keep your thinking your own
  • The Think-First Principle — a sequencing habit that separates AI use that sharpens you from AI use that hollows you out
  • Cognitive forcing functions and AI-free zones, borrowed from medicine and aviation
  • A complete 30-day challenge turning every idea into a repeatable habit

Grounded entirely in cited research (MIT, SBS Swiss Business School, the American Psychological Association, and more) — for anyone who wants to work alongside AI without quietly becoming dependent on it.

Things Our Grandparents Knew Yog M Creations
BOOK · BY YOG MANISH

Things Our Grandparents Knew: 100 Forgotten Lessons for a Simpler, Smarter, and More Meaningful Life

100 forgotten lessons on time, money, food, work, relationships and home — worth carrying forward, not left behind.

What if the generations before us understood something about living well that we have slowly forgotten? Before smartphones filled every quiet moment, many people lived with a different rhythm — they saved what they could, repaired what they owned, cooked, grew, made, shared, waited, listened, and spent time together.

Across 100 thoughtful lessons, this book explores forgotten habits and principles across ten themes:

  • The wisdom of time — patience and appreciating ordinary moments
  • The wisdom of money — saving, repairing, and thoughtful buying
  • The wisdom of food — cooking simply and reducing waste
  • The wisdom of work — craftsmanship, responsibility, and pride in useful work
  • The wisdom of relationships, home, childhood, nature, character and living

Through stories, reflections, and practical ideas for modern life, it asks: what can we learn from the past without trying to live in it?
